# Cinder — audit, 2026-09-18

**Against `637e804` (v0.3.8), with the fixes it produced landing in `dde1ec0`.** Every number here
was measured on this tree today; where something is inference it says so.

The audit was asked for alongside two pieces of work — SensMe channels, and Flint's feature gap
against Sony's Music Center — so it is organised around what those touched, plus a sweep of the
things that can only be seen by running the gates and reading across the seams. Three of its four
findings are fixed; the rest is recorded here rather than acted on, with a reason each time.

## Part B — Found and fixed

### B1. The battery gauge chased the terminal voltage, and a single sagged sample could switch the player off

**Reported by the owner, 2026-09-18: 22%, then 58% seconds after plugging the charger in, then 61%.**

What the app did: `read_battery()` reads `/sys/class/power_supply/battery/capacity` every 10 s and
publishes it, unsmoothed, to the status bar, the Device screen and `battery_guard`.

What the hardware does:

* There is **no fuel gauge on this platform** (`analysis/RE_battery.md`): no coulomb counter, no
  `current_now`, no `charge_full`. Confirmed again today from the kernel's own config
  (`cinder-sony-analysis` `device/nw-a55/…/kernel/config.txt`): `CONFIG_MTK_SMART_BATTERY` is unset
  and **every** MediaTek state-of-charge source — `SOC_BY_HW_FG`, `SOC_BY_SW_FG`, `SOC_BY_AUXADC`,
  `SOC_BY_EXT_HW_FG` — is off. The capacity comes from Sony's `bq24262_wmport` driver, which has
  terminal voltage and nothing else.
* The repo's own 123-sample log (`artifacts/session/battery_track.tsv`) shows it: `capacity` tracks
  `voltage_now` with **r = 0.96**, at **4.65 mV per percentage point** across 83–100%. Of 25 capacity
  changes in that log, 20 move in the same direction as the voltage in the same interval. The
  mid-range of this chemistry is flatter than the top, so a point there is worth less than 4.65 mV.

So two ordinary things move the *reading* by tens of points without the charge moving at all:

* **Load.** Amp, screen and Bluetooth together sag the cell by tens of millivolts, and it recovers
  when they stop.
* **The charger.** Plugging one in puts the charger's regulation voltage, less the drop across the
  cell's own resistance, on the terminals immediately — before any charge has gone in.

The owner's sequence is the second on top of the first: 36 points arriving in the time it takes to
push a plug in, which no battery does. 36 points here is roughly 90–170 mV depending on where on the
curve it sits, which is unremarkable for a small cell meeting a charger.

**The severity is not the display.** `battery_guard` powers the device off at `pct <= 3` on one
sample, deliberately, because `/contents` is unjournalled vfat and a flat battery mid-write is the
corruption path that bricked this device once. A sag that reads 2% while the cell is really at 15%
would have taken the player down in the middle of a track.

**Fixed** (`cinder-home/src/main.cpp`): the reported level is slew-limited to one point per poll —
6 points a minute, about thirty times faster than this device can actually discharge, so nothing
true is hidden. The status bar, the Device screen and the guard all read that one level, so they
cannot say different numbers. A divergence of 5 points or more logs one line carrying the raw value
*and* `voltage_now`, which is the measurement a device session needs to calibrate the sag.

Three harness scenarios pin it: `batt-dip` (a transient dip to 2% moves the reported level 40 → 37
and never reaches the power-off path), `batt-plug` (the cable lands and the reading jumps 22 → 58;
no single step is bigger than one point, and the level still climbs toward it) and `batt-flat` (a
sustained flat battery still powers off, within a minute). The three existing low-battery scenarios
still pass unchanged. The divergence log line now names the direction and whether a charger is
attached, because those two together say which of the causes above it was.

**Left honest:** if the first reading after a boot is a sagged one it seeds there and walks up at a
point per 10 s. There is no way to tell a sagged reading from a real one at t=0 without an OCV table
this project does not have.

### B2. A glyph's pixels depended on which screen had been drawn before it

Found by the golden gate, while adding screens that have nothing to do with text rendering: three
`uiscale_*` previews changed, and the change was 48 pixels of a single "F".

`text.rs`'s glyph cache keyed on the size **quantised to 0.25 px**, on the reasoning that the sizes
in use are a small fixed set. They are — until the UI scale multiplies them (80/100/120/140%), after
which two roles a quarter-pixel apart share a bucket and **the first rasterisation into that bucket
wins for both**. Two consequences, in order of importance:

1. **At UI scale 140% some text was drawn at the wrong size.** Not a rounding artefact: a subtitle
   role rasterised at a neighbouring role's size. Re-keying on the exact size moved 20,084 pixels of
   `uiscale_140_sound` — the screen now renders its subtitles at the size it asks for.
2. **The render was not a pure function of the screen**, so "this change moved no pixels" was a
   weaker claim than it looked, and the golden gate could be perturbed by unrelated work.

**Fixed:** the key is `size.to_bits()`. The cache was always bounded by the distinct (font, char,
size) triples actually drawn; this adds at most one entry per size that was previously sharing.
Four `uiscale_*` previews are re-blessed **because they are now correct**, not because they moved.

### B3. SensMe: the research was finished and the player could not use it

`analysis/RE_sensme_musiccenter.md` records the whole path — the engine driven from our own code
(§5), the player's parser and akeys (§6), the FLAC container (§7), and **two device proofs** that
the scanner turns these tags into channels (§9 FLAC, §10 MP3). `docs/PLAN_sensme_sync.md` lists
M1/M2 — Cinder reading the akeys, and the channel screens — as the next step.

Nothing in `player/` mentioned SensMe. A grep for `sensme|SMFM|smfmf` across `player/`,
`cinder-home/` and `installer/` returned nothing at all. The device work was done and the app had
not been told.

**Fixed** in `dde1ec0`: `cinder-db` reads the akeys, the library builds the channels, there is a
two-level SensMe screen, and `sensme` is an opt-in install component. The part that matters for this
audit is the shape it was given: **Cinder reads what the player's scanner computed, never the tag**,
so Sony's Music Center and Flint are the same to it, and a test asserts that rather than a comment
claiming it.

### B3a. With nothing plugged in, the Device screen said `FAULT 2`

Reported by the owner the same day. The Battery row's charger label reads the bq24262's STATUS
register: bits 6:4 are the state, bits 2:0 a fault code, and `charger_label` shows any non-zero code
as `FAULT n` — correctly, while a charger is attached.

With the cable **out** the part has no VBUS and puts a non-zero code in that field. Nothing is wrong
with the player; the charger is idle because there is nothing to charge from. Cinder was reporting
a hardware fault on every device that was not plugged in.

**Fixed:** the shell, which is the only place holding both the charger register and the
charger-detect nodes, marks the state "no input" when `usb/online` and `dc/online` are both 0 — the
same reading `battery_guard` already trusts to decide whether to switch the device off — and the
screen renders that as `ON BATTERY`. The fault field is not decoded and is not guessed at (there is
no datasheet for this part here): the raw registers still go to the footer untouched, and a fault
code seen on battery is logged once, so nothing is hidden, it just stops being called a fault.

A fault while a charger IS attached still shows as before; the test asserts both halves.

### B4. Two structural drift checks, run today

* **Every component in `components.conf` is handled by `install_cinderhome.sh`** — all nine,
  checked by name. This is the one place where adding a line to a catalogue silently does nothing.
* **Every `#[no_mangle]` export in `cinder-ffi` is declared in `cinder.h` — except one.**
  `cinder_font_probe` is declared ad hoc inside `cinder-home/src/probe.cpp` instead. It is a
  diagnostic used by `cinder-probe --fontchain` and the two declarations agree today, so this is
  drift rather than a defect: the header is supposed to be the whole FFI surface, and a second place
  to declare one is a second place for a signature to disagree. Not fixed — it belongs with whoever
  next touches the probe.

Both checks are one-liners and worth putting in CI; neither is today.

## Part C — Found, not fixed

### C1. An unreadable battery reads as 100%

`read_battery()` returns **100** when every candidate sysfs path fails. That is a safe choice for the
*guard* (an unreadable device must never be switched off by a guess, and `battery_charging()` makes
the same assumption) and a false statement on the *status bar*, which will draw a full battery
forever on a device that cannot answer.

The honest display is a battery with no fill and no number — Cinder already draws "no data" states
elsewhere and says so. Left alone here because it is a UI change to a screen element every other
screen composes, and this audit's battery work is already device-unverified.

### C2. The channel names are inferred, and the screen prints them as fact

`SENSME_CHANNELS` — Active, Emotional, Lounge, Dance, Extreme, Upbeat, Relax, Mellow, Morning,
Daytime, Evening, Night, Midnight — is the order Sony's own player's channel thumbnails appear in its
image atlas (RE note §10). The id → name table in `libMediaStoreService.so` holds ids, not names, so
**the mapping is unverified**. Five tracks' bitmasks are consistent with it, which is not proof.

It is deliberately the only place in Cinder that knows a channel's name, so correcting it is a
one-array change, and `DEVICE_CHECKLIST.md` 14.2 now says how: open the stock SensMe screen for a
track whose bitmask is known and read which channel it is in.

### C3. Branch protection still does not bind the owner

`AUDIT_2026-09-01` §D5 and `PLAN_2026-09-14` A4/B0 #3 both raise it. Checked again today: the last
five workflow runs on `main` are all `event: push` by the owner. CI is green on all of them, so this
remains latent rather than active — but it is the finding most likely to matter on the day something
lands that a review would have caught. **Owner's decision, one API call.**

### C4. Documentation drift, in three places

* `CLAUDE.md`'s gate counts — Part A1.
* **`docs/README.md` does not index `docs/PLAN_sensme_sync.md`.** That file is the live plan for the
  work this audit accompanies, and `docs/README.md`'s own claim is that it indexes every document in
  `docs/`. Every other file there is listed.
* `PLAN_sensme_sync.md` §6 still reads "M1 + M2 in Cinder — 2 sessions" as outstanding. It is not,
  as of `dde1ec0`.

### C5. The sabi is read and not used

`cinder-db` returns the chorus position for every analysed track, because it costs nothing to read
alongside the channel bitmask. Sony's own SensMe player starts a channel track *at* the sabi.
Cinder does not, deliberately: a seek applied at every track start interacts with resume, the
scrobbler's "played enough to count" rule and the queue, and none of that should be introduced in the
same change as the screens. `PLAN_sensme_sync.md` M2 already describes it as optional and default-on;
it should stay off until the screens have been used on a device.
