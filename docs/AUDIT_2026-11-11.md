# Audit, 2026-11-11: stability, the network code, and what the hardware still offers

A fresh pass over the whole project at `1c3b328` (v0.4.1 plus the day's cast fixes), on the owner's
NW-A55 running the dev build on Sony's 1.02. Three questions: do the existing setups hold up, is
the code added since the last full audit (2026-10-18: everything Wi-Fi) sound, and what can this
hardware still do that a current player does and Cinder does not.

Done in two rounds on the same day. The second went deeper: every parser fuzzed (as 32-bit ARM
code too), the C++ decoders under sanitizers, the player probed over its own Wi-Fi, the bundled
network programs' versions, and the kernel's configuration. The fixes that followed are in the
working tree, and §5 says which finding each one closes.

## 1. Result in short

- **Every gate passes:** all 29 that can run on a Linux PC; CI and edge are green on GitHub.
- **The player is healthy:** no kernel faults, no failures in the day's log, 143 MB free with
  Wi-Fi and the phone remote up.
- **Fuzzing:** 2.4 million damaged files and network answers through every parser found two ways
  to stop the player (a skin's text file, a font's character map). 7,500 malformed requests at the
  three servers, and 870,000 rounds through the C++ stream parsers and decoders under sanitizers,
  found nothing.
- **Nine defects fixed**, each with a test where one is possible, and installed on the player
  the same evening (§5).
- **Three things left open that need a decision,** not a patch: network AAC is decoded by the
  firmware's 2019 decoder inside the Home app; OpenSSL and wpa_supplicant are behind; and why
  joining Wi-Fi needs five scans.
- **Openings** are ranked in §6. The cheapest are in the sound path; the biggest surprise is that
  the kernel already has everything for USB sound out to an external DAC.

## 4. Fuzzing

Why it matters: the player's build aborts on any panic (`panic = "abort"`), and everything below
runs inside the Home app. A panic or a hang reachable from a file or a network answer takes the
Home app down, and the player restarts when its Home app dies. If the file is read at every start
(a chosen skin, a font), that is a restart loop until the boot ladder gives up and starts Sony's
player.

All of it was throwaway test code, removed afterwards. Inputs were real samples of each format,
damaged at random: cut short, bits flipped, pieces repeated or deleted, lengths pushed to their
limits, bytes that are not UTF-8.

| What | How much | Found |
|---|---|---|
| The phone remote, the drive share (all fourteen WebDAV verbs), the UPnP renderer (description, every SOAP action, event subscriptions), over TCP | 7,500 requests | nothing |
| 25 groups of parsers, called directly: covers (JPEG, PNG, BMP), Winamp skins (the zip, its bitmaps, its text files), tags and lyrics in FLAC, MP3, M4A, M4B and Ogg, `.lrc`, cue sheets, playlists, the stats, audiobook, likes and shared-folder files, palettes, saved views, the radio list, the Wi-Fi and radio status text, JSON answers (LRCLIB, MusicBrainz, radio-browser, GitHub, Last.fm, Chromecast), UPnP XML, mDNS, Cast frames, HTTP heads and paths, the PCM tap | 1.5 million inputs on a PC, 857,000 more as **32-bit ARM code** under qemu (the player's own word size, where a length can wrap) | **finding 4** (skin colour), the same on both |
| A font put on the drive (`cinder_font.ttf`) | 20 inputs, then eight chosen ones | **finding 5** (a load that never ends). Cut-off copies, a zeroed block and random bytes are all refused or loaded at once |
| The C++ stream code, built with address and undefined-behaviour sanitizers, every input in an exactly sized buffer: HLS playlists, MPEG-TS, ADTS, fragmented MP4, ICY titles | 600,000 rounds | nothing |
| The same for FLAC (dr_flac 0.13.3), WAV headers and MP3 (minimp3) as the radio feeds them | 270,000 rounds | nothing |

Also measured for every input: the slowest call (nothing over 0.2 s apart from the font) and the
largest single allocation (3.9 MB, a FLAC picture block), since a length field that asks for a
gigabyte is an out-of-memory kill on a 467 MB player.

Not fuzzed: the cast file server and the Chromecast session as running servers (their parsers
were), the remote's library pages with a library loaded, and the firmware's AAC decoder (finding 8).

Also read and found sound: the setuid Wi-Fi helper (fixed paths, a verb only, passwords on stdin
and wiped, SSIDs as hex), both curl callers (the bundled curl speaks only http, https, ws and
ipfs, so no playlist can point it at a file or another protocol; the updater is HTTPS only), the
update download (checked against the release's SHA-256 before anything is placed), and the
script-gated font fallback that keeps a network title with an emoji from loading an 80 MB font.

## 5. Findings

"Fixed" means changed in the working tree with the gates passing, and installed on the owner's
player that evening (dev build); the State column says what was then seen there. "Open" needs a
decision or a measurement first.

| # | Finding | How established | State |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Settings ▸ Storage shows no SD card when the card mounts late.** The card's size was read once, when the library is built (2.1 s in); that boot the card mounted at 4.7 s. A race: on the boot after the fix the card was mounted in time | Log (`SD 0 MB`, a 238 GB card), code, and seen on the panel | **Fixed:** read again whenever storage is reported, and reported the first time the card is seen mounted. Test in `nav.rs`. On the player the bar is there; the late case did not recur to be seen fixed |
| 2 | **Installing an update on the player did not look at the battery.** A flash that loses power part-way is the one failure the boot ladder cannot rescue. Sony's updater programs on the player hold no battery test either (their strings were searched) | Code | **Fixed:** refused under 30% with no cable in. Test in `nav.rs` |
| 3 | **The phone remote served one connection at a time.** An idle connection (browsers open spares) held the next request 15 s; a song being sent held everything | Measured on the player: 14.9 s against 0.1 s | **Fixed:** a thread per connection, eight at most, one upload at a time. Test in `remote.rs`. **Seen on the player: 0.02 s** behind one idle connection and behind three; ten idle ones get the ninth a 503 at once |
| 4 | **A Winamp skin could end the Home app.** A colour in `pledit.txt` (or a tape's `textcolor`) with a character outside ASCII in its first six bytes, which any Latin-1 skin read as UTF-8 can have, was sliced through the middle of a character. With that skin chosen it happens at every start | Fuzzing, on a PC and as ARM code | **Fixed:** `skin.rs` `hex_color`. Test beside it; the fuzz that found it is clean after (60,000 inputs) |
| 5 | **A font on the drive could hang the player at every start.** fontdue visits every character a font's map claims; one damaged 12-byte group claiming four billion did not finish in ten minutes on a PC | Fuzzing; the input examined | **Fixed:** `text.rs` `cmap_is_sane` refuses a map claiming more characters than Unicode has, before the font is parsed. Test beside it; the input that hung is refused at once |
| 6 | **Soundscapes were one sound in the tests and another on the player.** Rain, Storm, Stream and Fire drew two random numbers inside one call; gcc (the tests) and clang (the player) evaluate a call's arguments in opposite orders. With the tests' seed, the fire's brightness check read −10.7 dB under gcc and −0.8 dB under clang, outside the spec. Over 14 seeds both compilers give −6 to −12 dB, so the sound itself was sound; the tests were not testing it | The self-tests built with clang; output hashed under both | **Fixed:** one draw a line, in the order the tests always measured (gcc's output is bit-identical to before, clang's now equals it), and `selftests.sh` runs under both compilers |
| 7 | **Wi-Fi said it failed just before it connected.** 36.6 s from interface up to link ready at −69 dBm, against a 30 s limit. The kernel log shows five scans, 5 s apart, before the network was tried at all | The player's kernel log | **Fixed** in part: the limit is 60 s. The join after the install took 7 s, so joins vary and the long ones are the open part. **Open:** why the first four scans sometimes pick nothing. `wpa_supplicant`'s debug output for one join would say; remembering the network's channel (`scan_freq`) would cut the join to a few seconds either way |
| 8 | **Network AAC is decoded by the firmware's own decoder, inside the Home app.** Internet radio and "Play to this player" hand AAC to Sony's `libFraunhoferAAC.so`: built with a 2016 compiler, shipped 2019, so without the fixes published for that decoder since. The streams come from any station (radio-browser's list is public) and, with "Play to this player" on, from anyone on the network. MP3 and FLAC go through current decoders | Code and the library's own strings | **Open.** Either decode in a child process (a crash then costs the song, not the Home app), or build the current decoder as `cinder-aac` already builds the encoder |
| 9 | **Two bundled network programs are behind.** OpenSSL 3.0.15 is from September 2024, and the 3.0 series left support on 2026-09-07. wpa_supplicant 2.10 is from 2022; 2.11 fixes, among others, a way to skip the inner login on an enterprise network saved without a server name to check (CVE-2023-52160), which is what a three-field line in `cinder_wifi.txt` saves. curl (8.22.0) and the CA list (2026-10-20) are current | Version output on the player | **Open:** a rebuild of the Wi-Fi option and a join test on a player |
| 10 | **Log lines that read as faults.** `input: still ZERO events … (foreign grab?)` on an untouched player; the dev discovery dump called `arecord`, which the player lacks | The day's log | **Fixed:** reworded (seen in the player's new log); `/proc/asound/pcm` instead. Left alone: the second `StopBootAnimation`, which is a deliberate sweep, and Sony's `killall` complaint under it |
| 11 | **Documents behind.** `ROADMAP.md` listed a finished item under "Being built next" and said speakers were tested "against simulated devices only" | Read | **Fixed** |
| 12 | **Two edge builds were published for one commit** (`2198d89`): one from the push hook's clone, one from `tools/edge.sh` run in the working tree, whose builds collided with a dev build there | The working tree's `dist/stable` time, the hook's logs | None needed: the lock added in `1c3b328` covers builds in one tree |
| 13 | **The cast fixes of 2026-11-11 are installed and unseen:** dragging to seek, Track information, the cover and heart changing on a skip | Host tests only | Two minutes with a speaker |

Smaller, not changed: a FLAC file with an ID3 tag in front has its lyrics read but not its series

## 6. What the hardware and the firmware still offer

Read from the running player: the kernel's configuration (`/proc/config.gz`), the codec's mixer
controls, the I2C buses, the input nodes, the real-time clock and the USB controller. Sizes are
working days for one person, to a build ready for a device test.

### Sound: three features at a hook that already exists

`libcinder_mono.so` is loaded into Sony's sound service and already rewrites every buffer on its
way to the jack and to Bluetooth, after Sony's effects and before the volume (it sums to mono and
mixes soundscapes there). Anything that is arithmetic on two channels fits the same place.

| # | Feature | What a current player does | Why it fits here | Size |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **ReplayGain** (track and album) | Evens out loudness between songs from their tags, without the pumping of a compressor (which is what Dynamic Normalizer is) | One multiply per sample at the hook; the tag readers written for lyrics already read FLAC and ID3 comments | 1 |
| 2 | **Crossfeed** | Feeds a little of each channel to the other, delayed and filtered, so hard-panned recordings stop being tiring on headphones | Two short filters per channel at the hook; works on Bluetooth as well | ½ |
| 3 | **Parametric EQ, with headphone profiles** | Loads a published correction for a named headphone (AutoEQ's text format) instead of ten fixed bands | On the jack the codec's five hardware filters already take coefficients (the DAC EQ writes them), at no processor cost; for Bluetooth the same filters run at the hook | 1 to 2 |

Not offered: crossfade. It needs two decoders running at once, and Sony's player has one.

### The network: the library stops ending at the SD card

Wi-Fi, an HTTPS client, decoders for MP3, AAC, FLAC and WAV, and a player that takes a web address
all exist (internet radio and "Play to this player" use them).

| # | Feature | Why it fits here | Size |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | **Browse and play a media server on the home network** (MusicBee, a NAS, Plex, Jellyfin: UPnP ContentDirectory) | The discovery and SOAP client are in `cast.rs`; only the browsing pages and one action are new | 2 |
| 5 | **A Subsonic client** (Navidrome and the rest), with "keep on the card" | The same playback path; the API is plain HTTPS and JSON. Keeping albums on the card for offline use is the part other small players lack | 3 to 5 |
| 6 | **Podcasts** | A feed is XML over HTTPS; an episode is an MP3 link; the audiobook code already remembers a position | 2 to 3 |
| 7 | **Spotify Connect** | `librespot` (MIT; a Premium account is needed) can write decoded sound to a pipe, which is how AirPlay already reaches the player. Its own folder and LICENSE | 1 to 2 |
| 8 | **A name instead of an address** (`cinder.local`) for the phone remote and the drive share | Today a phone types an address and `:8080`. The Chromecast code already sends mDNS questions; answering one is the other half | ½ |

Already on the list and still the cheapest of all: the cover sent to a cast speaker (1 hour).

### Hardware and kernel features nothing uses yet

| # | Feature | Evidence | Size |
|---|---|---|---|
| 9 | **USB sound out to an external DAC** | The kernel has all of it built in: the host controller and OTG (`USB_MTK_HDRC_HCD`, `USB_MTK_OTG`), the USB audio class driver (`SND_USB_AUDIO`), and a root hub is already listed. No module to build. What is unproven is the WM-PORT side: a host cable, power to the DAC, and switching the port to host | a cable first; then 1 to 2 for the output route |
| 10 | **Compressed swap** as a cushion against out-of-memory restarts | `ZRAM` is built in, `/sys/block/zram0` exists unused, and the player's busybox has `mkswap` and `swapon`. An out-of-memory kill of the Home app is a restart; 64 MB of compressed swap makes a spike (a large font, Wi-Fi and a server at once) slow instead of fatal. To measure: the processor cost when it is used | ½, plus a test |
| 11 | **An NFC sticker starts an album or playlist** | The reader (`cxd224x`) reads tags as well as pairing headphones | 1 to 2 |
| 12 | **Line out**, a fixed level for an amplifier or a car's aux input | The codec's `output device` has `line` and `fixedline`; Cinder never sets that control (Sony's services choose). Needs a confirm, and to switch itself off when the plug is pulled, because full level into earphones is dangerous. What each setting does at the jack has to be measured first | ½, plus the measurement |
| 13 | **An alarm: wake and play** | The real-time clock has a wake alarm (`/sys/class/rtc/rtc0/wakealarm`, wake-up enabled) and the player already suspends to RAM. To prove: that it wakes the player from suspend, and from power off | 1 to 2 |
| 14 | **A music folder on a NAS, mounted** | The kernel has FUSE (the SD card's exFAT uses it) and no CIFS or NFS, so a share would come through a user-space client. Sony's scanner would then index it like the card; how slow that is over Wi-Fi is the question | unknown |
| 15 | **A USB keyboard or remote** through the same host cable | `USB_HID` and `HID_GENERIC` are built in | with 9 |
| 16 | **Screen turned 180°**, as a switch | There is no accelerometer on any I2C bus (MediaTek's sensor entries are empty stubs), so it cannot be automatic | ½ |

### Not possible on this player

So that nobody spends a day finding out again:

- **LE Audio (LC3), aptX Adaptive or Lossless:** the Bluetooth controller is a 4.2 part; LDAC,
  aptX HD, AAC and SBC XQ are the ceiling, and all four are in.
- **RDS on FM:** the tuner (Si4708) has no RDS decoder.
- **Automatic screen rotation, step counting:** no motion sensor.
- **Recording from a microphone:** the codec has microphone inputs, and the player has no
  microphone; only Sony's noise-cancelling earphones bring one.
- **An ALSA loopback** for routing one program's sound into another: `SND_ALOOP` is not built, and
  would be a kernel module.

Not looked into: Tidal and Qobuz. Both need a developer agreement before a third-party player may
log in, so they are a question for the owner before they are an engineering one.

## 7. Suggested order

1. The device checks only a person can do: the phone remote from a phone's browser, a soundscape
   by ear, and the cast checks of finding 13.
2. Decide finding 8 (the AAC decoder) and schedule finding 9 (OpenSSL, wpa_supplicant) with it:
   both end in a rebuild of the Wi-Fi option.
3. ReplayGain, then crossfeed: 1½ days for two features people ask every player for, on the jack
   and on Bluetooth.
4. The media-server browser: 2 days, and it works with a home MusicBee on day one.
5. Buy or borrow a WM-PORT host cable: the kernel side of USB sound out is already there.
